PDBSplat

Proteins from the Protein Data Bank, rendered in the browser as Gaussian splats. Each atom becomes a soft blob of colour at its place in the structure, so a protein reads as a cloud of matter rather than as balls and sticks.

Open App GitHub

Examples

The app opens on crambin, and its panel lists five more examples, from small to large. Any other entry in the Protein Data Bank loads by its four-character ID.

  • 1CRN: crambin, a small plant protein of 327 atoms
  • 1BNA: a short stretch of DNA
  • 1EJG: crambin again, with its hydrogens, alternate conformations and the directions each atom moves in
  • 1D3Z: ubiquitin, as solved by NMR, in several models
  • 4HHB: human haemoglobin
  • 1IGT: an intact mouse antibody of almost 13,000 atoms, four protein chains in a Y

Styles

  • Splats: soft Gaussian blobs, each sized by its element's van der Waals radius, with a Hardness control that firms their edges from a soft falloff to a flat disc.
  • Spheres: hard-edged splats shaded as spheres. At full size and opacity they make a space-filling model, cutting into one another along curved creases.
  • Surface: a smooth molecular surface, meshed by marching cubes in a background thread, on which the atoms' colours meet along clean boundaries.
  • Field: the atoms' density, ray-marched as a cloud, denser where atoms crowd and fading away at the structure's edge.

Colour and Detail

  • Colour by element, by chain, by B-factor, which says how far each atom moves about its place, by a rainbow along each chain, or by secondary structure. Burial darkens each atom by how deeply the atoms around it bury it.
  • Point at an atom to name it, with its residue, chain, B-factor and occupancy, and pick it to focus on its residue or chain.
  • NMR models show the first, all of them at once, so that where they disagree reads as a cloud, or each in turn as an animation.
  • Thermal motion blurs each atom by its B-factor, or draws it as its thermal ellipsoid, stretched along the way it moves most where the entry records that.
  • Levels of detail merge the atoms into one blob per residue or per chain as you zoom out.
  • Focus on the ligands and the residues that hold them, or on the backbone, and fade the rest.
  • Blend each splat over what's behind it, as a glow that reads like an X-ray, or weighted, so nothing depends on the order the splats are drawn in.
  • Scene effects: a depth cue, a clipping plane to see inside, bloom, and ambient occlusion.

Built With

TypeScript, React, three.js and React Three Fiber, built with Vite. The splats are drawn by three.js's WebGPU-based Gaussian splat rendering, with a WebGL 2 fallback in browsers without WebGPU or on GPUs where it fails. Entries are fetched from the RCSB as mmCIF files and read by the app's own parser.

Credits

Protein structures come from the RCSB Protein Data Bank. The element colours are Jmol's CPK palette, and the van der Waals radii are mostly A. Bondi's (1964).
