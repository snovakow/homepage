Monty Hall Problem

A 3D game show in the browser, played on the Monty Hall problem: three crates, one prize, and the question of whether to switch.

Open App Make a Show as a Gift GitHub

The Problem

You pick one of three crates, and one of them holds a prize. The host, who knows where the prize is, then opens one of the other two, always an empty one, and offers you the choice: stay with your pick, or switch to the other closed crate.

Switching wins two times in three. Your first pick is right only one time in three, and when it's wrong, the host has to leave the prize in the crate you'd switch to.

The puzzle is named for Monty Hall, the original host of the TV game show Let's Make a Deal. It became famous in 1990, when Marilyn vos Savant answered it in Parade magazine, and thousands of readers wrote in to insist the answer was wrong.

The Show

  • A host runs the show round after round: she welcomes you, explains the rules, announces each round's prize, opens an empty crate and offers you the switch. She dances when you win and sulks when you lose.
  • A tab adds up what you win, and keeps count of how staying and switching have each paid off.
  • A simpler game is a straight pick of one crate in three.
  • Shows as gifts. Make a show for someone, with a greeting to them in lights over the stage and the winnings kept within a range you choose, and send it to them as a link. Everything about a gift lives in the link itself, so nothing is stored on a server.
  • A studio audience murmurs, applauds, cheers and groans along.
  • A night studio under spotlights, with a glossy floor that reflects the stage and beams of light through a haze. A win sets off confetti and jets of flame, and the finale ends in fireworks. The confetti and the fireworks are moved by the graphics card, thousands of pieces at a time.
  • Made for phones first, with buttons big enough for a thumb and a layout for each way the phone is held.

Built With

TypeScript, React, three.js and React Three Fiber, built with Vite. The effects are written for three.js's WebGPU renderer, in its node materials and compute passes, and drawn with WebGL 2 on every device, or with WebGPU itself by adding ?webgpu to the address.
